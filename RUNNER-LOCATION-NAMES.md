# Automatic runner location names

Publicly available runners resolve their GPS coordinates with the installed Expo Location native reverse geocoder on phones, or Photon on browsers. The runner availability panel and the buyer's nearby-runner list show a pin and city/street label; Expo Go marker callouts also include it. Working-area profile preferences remain separate from the live location.

- No new API key or dependency is required for Android/iOS. Street coverage depends on the device's geocoding service. When a city is missing, a returned district/subregion can identify the area; missing streets are never invented. Building numbers and full postal addresses are not stored.
- The first accepted public GPS fix triggers a lookup. Subsequent lookups are limited to one per 30 seconds after at least 40 metres of movement, or one per two minutes while stationary. Failed lookups retry on later accepted GPS fixes. Only one native lookup runs at a time.
- GPS publishing happens first. Geocoder/publishing failures are isolated, and a native background task waits at most ten seconds for this optional work. Results older than 30 seconds are discarded. Foreground sharing works in Expo Go; background sharing keeps its existing development-build and permission requirements.
- Convex accepts labels only from the authenticated, enabled runner's current public session. It rejects out-of-order results and results over 100 metres from the latest GPS fix. Labels are hidden after movement over 100 metres, three minutes of newer GPS evidence, going offline, or starting a new session. Existing records without labels continue to work.
- Availability remains governed by the existing 45-second GPS lease. Private delivery GPS does not publish a public street address. The nearby-runner response exposes only the label, not address-session metadata.
- Web now resolves its own public GPS fixes using [Photon](https://photon.komoot.io/), an OpenStreetMap geocoder. Only latitude/longitude and lookup options are sent, with cookies omitted; account and session identifiers stay with Convex. Queries cover a nearby 100-metre area and abort after eight seconds. The same 30-second moving/two-minute stationary throttle applies. Photon is a public service without an availability guarantee, suitable for the small project demonstration. A Photon-compatible endpoint can be configured with `EXPO_PUBLIC_GEOCODER_URL` (restart Expo after changing it). Browser labels include OpenStreetMap attribution.
- No third-party lookup runs for private delivery tracking. Missing coverage, denied GPS permission, browser GPS inaccuracy, provider errors or an offline browser may leave labels unavailable; available map positions remain usable. Phone geocoding stays unchanged.

Device walkthrough: reload the app, go offline and online as a runner, grant location permission, and wait for a fresh fix. Check the pin/label in the availability panel. On a nearby buyer account, use My location and compare the runner's label in the list and marker callout. Walk to another street, allow at least 30 seconds, and check it updates. Go offline and confirm the runner disappears. Street names are approximate and must be checked on real devices; automated tests use simulated geocoder results.
